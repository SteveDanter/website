3.0HDi V6 Exclusive 5d Auto 2011

My favourite car

I wasn't actually planning to buy a car that day.

Admittedly, the Laguna was getting tired and really did need replacing, but I'd gone back to Trade Centre Wales without any firm idea of what I wanted. A salesman eventually approached and asked what I was looking for.

"An estate car."

He was almost dismissive. Estate cars? Who wants those? They're over in the far corner, mate.

So I started walking towards the far corner.

On the way, though, I was distracted by a sleek-looking silver estate car. It was a Citroën C5 Tourer and I immediately liked the look of it.

I took it out for a test drive. The low-fuel warning was flashing away in the middle of the dashboard, so I didn't go very far and certainly didn't put my foot down. For some reason I was convinced I was driving the 2.0 diesel version.

I liked it enough to buy it.

Getting permission

There was, of course, one important part of the car-buying process still to complete.

I phoned Sharon and told her I'd found a car and was thinking of buying it. I always need her approval before buying something expensive. That way, if it subsequently turns out to be a complete lemon, I can remind her that she told me to buy it.

She agreed.

Excellent. Responsibility successfully shared.

It was only afterwards that I discovered I'd just bought the 3.0-litre V6 diesel.

I was over the moon.

Not quite the 2.0 diesel

The C5 had a 3.0-litre V6 producing around 240 horsepower. It would do 0–60 in roughly 7.6 seconds and had a quoted top speed of around 150mph.

For a large, comfortable Citroën estate car, it was ridiculously quick.

It was also an Exclusive, so it came with just about everything Citroën could throw at it. But outright performance wasn't really what made me love the car. It was the combination of performance and comfort. It could cover huge distances effortlessly, yet when you wanted the power it was immediately there.

It could even burn off the occasional boy racer.

With my caravan attached.

A new car needs a new caravan

Eventually the old caravan was traded in as well and I bought a newer one. Not exactly new, obviously, but considerably less old than the previous one and positively luxurious by comparison.

This one had such modern conveniences as a toilet and a cooker.

All I needed now was a towbar on the C5.

I bought one and fitted it myself out the back of the house. Looking back, I'm not entirely sure how I managed it. The 3.0 V6 had a massive twin exhaust system and silencer occupying what seemed like virtually the entire underside of the rear of the car, yet somehow all the towbar mounting points were accessible and everything fitted.

Once attached, the caravan presented very little challenge to the C5. With that V6 diesel pulling it, there was certainly no shortage of power.

Possibly responsible for the global oil crisis

I kept the C5 for about ten years.

During the early years I was still doing the sort of mileage I'd been doing for Practice Net, travelling around the country visiting clients. A 3.0-litre V6 diesel probably wasn't the most environmentally responsible choice for that sort of mileage and I suspect I may personally have made a measurable contribution to the global oil crisis.

But it was a wonderful way to travel.

Then, in November 2015, everything changed when I left Practice Net and joined CGI.

After spending years driving all over the country for work, my daily commute suddenly became about six miles.

I'd finally acquired the perfect long-distance motorway car just in time to stop travelling long distances.

Sharon takes over

Eventually I bought another motorbike and started using that for much of my commuting, so Sharon increasingly took over the C5.

Ironically, while my working life had become much more local, Sharon was travelling back and forth to Cardiff for her job, so the C5 once again found itself doing what it did best — effortlessly eating up miles.

By then it had stopped being simply "my company car" and had become part of the family. We'd travelled enormous distances in it, towed the caravan with it and kept it far longer than most of the cars I'd owned before.

Ten years later

I've owned faster vehicles since and I've certainly owned newer ones, but the Citroën C5 Tourer 3.0 V6 HDi Exclusive remains my favourite car of all time.

Perhaps that's partly because I kept it for so long, but I think it was simply an unusually good combination. It was fast without looking particularly fast, enormously comfortable, practical enough to carry everything I needed, powerful enough to tow the caravan without noticing and sophisticated enough to make long journeys almost effortless.

And to think I only found it because a salesman who didn't seem particularly impressed by my desire to buy an estate car pointed me towards the far corner of Trade Centre Wales.

Best of all, if it had turned out to be rubbish, I already had proof that Sharon had told me I could buy it.

Still moving.
