The Nissan Primera

After the Astras came a Nissan Primera, although it was never really intended to be my car. It had been Leeanne's company car and, when she left Practice Net to become a teacher, it was handed down to me while I waited for my new Hyundai Santa Fe to arrive.

I can't even remember for certain what engine it had. I think it was petrol, although most of the Practice Net fleet was diesel by then, so I wouldn't put money on it. What I do remember is that it was a definite step up in specification from the Astras and was a perfectly decent car. It just wasn't particularly memorable.

There is, however, one Primera memory that has stuck with me.

I was on my way to a client in Salisbury when a brilliant song I'd heard a couple of times before came on the radio: William Shatner's version of Pulp's Common People. Yes, Captain Kirk himself. And in this case, "sang" probably deserves the quotation marks.

I turned the stereo up past what any sensible person would regard as maximum and carried on towards the client.

Their offices were arranged around a quadrangle at the back, reached through a small entrance tunnel. I drove through the tunnel and into the car park with Captain Kirk still enthusiastically explaining the finer points of common people at full volume.

It was only when I stopped that I discovered something about the Nissan Primera that hadn't been mentioned in the brochure:

Cars aren't particularly soundproof.

My client, along with what appeared to be half his staff, was looking down from the open first-floor windows trying to work out what the hell the racket was.

Perhaps the Primera wasn't quite as forgettable as I thought.

Still moving.
