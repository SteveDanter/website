Kawasaki GPZ 1000 RX

An absolute beast of a bike

The driving force behind buying this bike was my good friend Vince—you remember him? I might have mentioned him once or twice. He was my best mate from my Army days in Germany and beyond. We both had bikes while we were out there and remained friends after leaving the Army.

One day, he asked if I fancied a two-week tour of southern Europe on our bikes. Duh! Of course I did. There was just one slight problem: I was driving an MG Metro Turbo at the time and didn’t actually own a bike.

The seed had been sown, though, so off I went to South Wales Superbikes and part-exchanged the Metro for the mighty GPZ.

On the way home from the dealer, a police motorcyclist pulled alongside me on the M4 and pointed at his speedometer before zooming off with his blue lights blazing. I glanced down at mine… 110 mph.

Different times.

Speed on the GPz was effortless. What a beast.

We set off on our European adventure a couple of weeks later and covered more than 2,000 miles, camping along the way. We travelled down the west coast of France to Biarritz, then headed east along the foot of the Pyrenees towards the south of France. From there, we rode northeast, stopping at Saint-Tropez—to ogle the topless ladies, naturally—before making a quick dash into Italy and Switzerland.

We couldn’t stay there for long because all we had were French francs, so we needed to be back in France before the next fuel stop. After that came a couple of nights in Germany’s Black Forest, followed by a journey west through France, Luxembourg, Belgium and the Netherlands. Eventually, we returned to France and caught the ferry from Calais back to Blighty.

All packed up and ready for our holidays

We couldn’t speak much French beyond oui and merci, so communicating with the locals proved difficult. This was the 1980s: no McDonald’s to fall back on and no self-service tills in the supermarkets. We had two choices—learn the language quickly or starve.

We starved.

I doubt we planned very much in advance. I imagine we simply rode until evening and camped wherever happened to be open.

I don’t remember suffering any of the discomfort I get on a bike nowadays—the numb bum, aching back and painful knees. Perhaps bikes were more comfortable then, or maybe I was simply younger and better able to put up with it.

We just rode and rode and rode. I don’t remember much of the journey in detail, but there was Nantes—twinned with Cardiff—Biarritz, Carcassonne, Saint-Tropez, Mont Blanc and the Black Forest. We took a few photographs, drank some bier and attempted to swim in mountain lakes. They were freezing, of course, being filled with meltwater running down from the snowy mountains.

The weather was certainly hot. I know this because Vince became so badly sunburnt on his back that he had to visit a pharmacy. The pharmacist took one look at him and actually swore—in English.

Later that day, probably after a bier or two, we decided to burst the tennis-ball-sized blisters on his back with our penknives. It was funny for us, watching Vince squirm. Not so much for Vince.

The route of our epic journey

We spent a lovely couple of days in the Black Forest, relaxing beside the river that flowed through the campsite and blasting around the region’s fantastic roads.

We decided to stay for a couple of nights so we could refuel our starved bodies. Between us, we had a basic grasp of German—or at least we should have had after spending three years in Germany with the Army. We knew words such as bier, frites, frikadelle, bratwurst, eins, zwei and drei. We could even manage entire sentences such as ein Bier, bitte.

All the important words, then.

That certainly wasn’t the last time I visited France on this bike. After settling down with my wife, we went camping in the Vendée a couple of times. We stayed in our little tent with nothing more than a Coleman Dual Fuel stove for making coffee and cooking. As I was reminded recently, I somehow conjured up garlic chicken on it. I can also remember steaming pasties.

One of the campsites was just around the corner from the famous Luna Parc travelling fairground. The site I mostly visit nowadays is also just across the road from Luna Parc. I’ve tried to work out exactly where we used to stay—and I’m usually pretty good at that sort of thing—but the area has changed so much over the years that I can’t quite place it.

The pitches seemed to be dotted randomly among the pine trees, and they were brilliant. The fallen pine needles made a lovely soft floor beneath the tent, with no muddy field to crawl out into each morning.

I have fond memories of nearly strangling some French kid with our strategically positioned clothesline one year.

Well, they should look where they’re going.

We would also make the obligatory visit to the zoo at La Palmyre to check on our relatives in the monkey enclosure. I wonder if that’s where the idea for Liam came from?

Anyway, the Kawasaki remained my wheels for the next couple of years. Eventually, I sold it and put the money towards the mortgage on my new marital home.

And that was the end of my biking days—for about 15 years. 🙁
