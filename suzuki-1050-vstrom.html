Suzuki V-Strom 1050 GT

The main photograph was taken when I went camping near Rhayader in mid-Wales—hence the excessive amount of luggage. I needed to be prepared for every possible weather condition that Wales might throw at me.

And I used all of it.

The V-Strom was another purchase from Thunder Road. I part-exchanged my Triumph Tiger Sport for it and, only a couple of days later, set off for a camping holiday near Rhayader.

When I reached the campsite, I pitched my little two-man tent. It had a blackout lining and had served me well during a trip to France the previous year. Unfortunately, I had packed it away while it was still wet. The lining was no longer quite as effective at blocking out the light—or, as I discovered later, the rain.

I had only just settled in when my camping neighbour returned on his own bright yellow Suzuki V-Strom 1050, although his wasn’t the GT version. We got chatting, went out for a couple of rides together later in the week and shared a few beers in the evenings.

It was also during this holiday that I discovered Biscoff ice cream—the moment when thousands of years of human culinary progress finally reached its logical conclusion. Nothing has tasted quite as good since, and it was all waiting for me in a little ice-cream shop in Aberdovey.

Yum yum. I really must go back one day—purely to confirm that it was every bit as magnificent as I remember. It’s important to verify these things.

The V-Strom itself was comfortable, capable and excellent for touring. It had cruise control and a wonderfully torquey engine, while the GT came equipped as standard with aluminium panniers and a top box. There was certainly no shortage of luggage space—which was just as well, judging by the photograph.

The digital display was rather disappointing considering what Suzuki could have fitted. It was only a basic monochrome LCD, although I suppose it displayed everything I actually needed to know.

So why complain, eh?

I covered a lot of miles on the Yellow Peril. It took me to France three times, carried me on numerous work trips and made the journeys to Leyland when John passed away.

Eventually, all those journeys began to create a problem. The bike was on a PCP agreement, and I was getting dangerously close to exceeding the permitted mileage.

So, it was back to Thunder Road.

As I am getting older, my intention was to downsize to something a little lighter and more manageable. With that in mind, I put down a deposit on a Honda XL750 Transalp.

We agreed on a part-exchange value for the V-Strom, and I also persuaded them to allow me enough additional mileage for one final holiday.

Where was I going?

You guessed it - France.

The plan was to spend a fortnight there, return home and then collect the new Transalp. The Yellow Peril still had one last European adventure ahead of it.
