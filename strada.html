Fiat Strada 65CL

My first car.

Learning to drive

I had passed my car test while I was still stationed at AAC Chepstow, sometime after completing my motorcycle test. Learning to drive may have been another of the “hobbies” available to us, although I think it was actually a course that we all completed rather than something I chose for one of my hobby slots.

Our lessons were provided by the Triangle School of Motoring, based in Bulwark, just across the River Wye from the camp.

The woman who owned the driving school had a pink Ford Mustang, which naturally made quite an impression on me.

Sadly, that wasn’t the car in which I took my lessons.

My learner car was something much more ordinary. In my memory it looked rather like a Ford Fiesta, although I’m fairly sure it wasn’t one. Whatever it was, it successfully did its job and I passed my test.

The pink Mustang would have made a much better story, though.

My first car

I was probably about 21 when I “bought” this silver Fiat Strada 65CL from my parents. It was my first car after leaving the Army, arriving sometime after my Suzuki GS400 motorcycle but before the Suzuki GS550L.

The agreed price was around £400, although “agreed” and “paid” are not necessarily the same thing. I doubt my parents ever received all their money.

In the mind of a first-time car owner, this thing was an absolute performance machine. It had the sportiest engine imaginable, handling that was second to none and tyres so enormously wide that it was a wonder they fitted on an ordinary road.

I think they were 145s.

Practically Formula One.

In reality, the Strada was—to put it kindly—a bit of a rust bucket. I could almost slide my hand down between the driver’s window and the outer door skin, reach the connecting rod and unlock it from the outside.

Who needed central locking?

None of that mattered. My cousin Terry and I used to tear around the local roads, him in his marginally sportier 1.3-litre Ford Escort and me in the mighty Strada.

I didn’t mind that his car was faster. I was obviously the better driver.

The biggest disadvantage was that the Fiat wasn’t rear-wheel drive, so I couldn’t drift it or perform doughnuts like the popular kids down at Llandow runway on a Saturday afternoon.

That was probably for the best. Given the amount of rust holding the car together, any serious sideways action might have resulted in parts of it continuing in several different directions.

Still, the Strada did assist with a certain amount of courting. There I was with my blond highlights, my arm hanging casually out of the window and slowly tanning in the sunshine.

I started seeing Karen, and we went everywhere together in my beautiful, if slightly ugly, Fiat Strada 65CL.

Mr and Mrs Danter

Karen and I once took the Strada to Saundersfoot, near Tenby, where we stayed in a bed and breakfast. The owner was rather strict about unmarried couples sharing a room, so for the duration of our stay we became Mr and Mrs Danter.

Our first unofficial married holiday—and considerably cheaper than a wedding.

Karen took this photograph of me lounging casually on the bonnet. There I am: about 21 years old, blond highlights gleaming in the sunshine and looking every inch the sophisticated Fiat Strada owner.

At the time, I probably thought I looked extremely cool.

I’ll leave others to decide whether I succeeded.

The Strada may not have been fast, glamorous or entirely structurally sound, but it gave me something far more important: independence. It was mine—or at least it would have been if I had ever finished paying my parents for it—and suddenly I could go wherever I wanted, whenever I wanted.

I don’t think I kept it for very long. Karen and I were still together when I owned the car that followed it, and the more I piece together where I was living and what was happening in my life, the more certain I become that my second car was the Ford Capri.

The Capri was sportier, rear-wheel drive and, most importantly, had considerably more room in the front for courting purposes.

Priorities.
