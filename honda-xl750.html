Honda XL750 Transalp

Possibly one of my favourite bikes, but also one of the most frustrating I’ve ever owned.

The Transalp was incredibly comfortable and ideally suited to long-distance riding. Unfortunately, the clutch and Euro 5 fuel mapping left a lot to be desired. It was a bike I could happily ride all day, even if it occasionally did its best to annoy me along the way.

Because Honda hadn’t fitted cruise control, I added an aftermarket system from Veridian Cruise. The original single-button version allowed me to rest my throttle hand on long journeys, although changing the set speed meant cancelling it and starting again. Veridian later introduced a three-button switch with speed adjustment and resume functions, which made it far more useful.

That’s enough about cruise control. The important thing is where the bike took me.

Back to France

In June 2024, I set off on what had become one of my regular road trips to France. My destination was La Pinède, a four-star holiday park at Les Mathes, near Royan on France’s Atlantic coast, where I would spend 11 nights in a luxury caravan.

I caught the ferry from Portsmouth to Ouistreham, arriving in France at about 10 p.m. From there, I had an hour’s ride to a Formule 1 hotel in Saint-Lô, where I stopped for the night.

The following morning, properly rested, I began a much more leisurely ride south towards Les Mathes.

I had stayed at La Pinède before and knew that it suited me perfectly. Although my visit was slightly outside the main holiday season, everything on the site was open. There was entertainment most evenings and even poolside entertainment—should I have felt energetic enough to participate.

I didn’t.

The weather wasn’t ideal, but I still managed to do everything I had travelled there to do: plenty of walking, relaxing beside the pool and finishing the day outside the bar with a nice cold 500 ml “pint” of Kronenbourg 1664.

There was also a quiz—in French.

I once managed to score 12 points, which I considered an outstanding achievement given that I probably didn’t understand most of the questions.

What more could you want?

I completed several walks of more than ten miles through the forest, around Les Mathes and along the surrounding footpaths. Eventually, I decided it was time to tackle a walk I had considered many times before: the route to the Phare de la Coubre lighthouse.

I had often seen the lighthouse in the distance and started walking towards it. The problem was that it is so tall that it can be seen from miles away. No matter how long I walked, it never seemed to get any closer.

This time, I was determined.

I finally reached the lighthouse, continued along the beach and then returned to the caravan through the forest along firebreak number 30. The complete walk was about 14 miles.

I had finally done it.

There were no encounters with live wild boar during my walks this time. I did find one dead and rather bloated, lying legs-up in a small watering hole, but that wasn’t quite the thrilling wildlife encounter I might have hoped for.

I also spotted a small deer darting back into the cover of the trees. Deer are considerably less alarming than wild boar—and generally smell better than that particular one did.

Spain and France with Liam

My next adventure began in September, when Liam and I set off on a tour together. We planned to catch the ferry from Portsmouth to Santander in northern Spain, then ride north along the west coast of France towards Ouistreham.

Rather than moving on every morning, we would stop at only two campsites—four nights at the first and three at the second. That would give us time to explore and enjoy the journey instead of spending the entire holiday packing, riding and unpacking.

We travelled to Santander aboard one of Brittany Ferries’ newer ships. It was apparently greener than the older vessels and was certainly quieter.

The sea was remarkably calm. I wouldn’t describe it as millpond smooth, but it wasn’t puke-inducingly rough either—which, for me, counts as an excellent crossing.
