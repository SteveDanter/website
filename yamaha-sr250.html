1983 – 1985

Yamaha SR250

I don’t actually have any photos of my own of this beast of a bike so here’s one off the internet.

What a step up

At the time I thought this was the ultimate bike, it was mega powerful, 21hp giving 81mph maximum speed, the stepped seat and raised bars made it look and feel like a Harley(not).

Even though it clearly doesn’t need it, I did of course customise the bike. I “borrowed” some steel conduit from the electrical workshops and crafted a sissy bar, it was a spectacular piece of engineering. I used my newly learned pipe bending skills to shape a piece of black steel tube on the pipe bender through 180 degrees to form an upside down U and then attached it to the grip rail with a couple of jubilee clips and some electrical tape.

I had this bike for the rest of my time at AAC Chepstow. It made the journey home a bit easier, I could almost keep up with the traffic on the motorway now.

Some of the other bikes on camp included a Yamaha RD250LC, a Suzuki X5 I have a vague recollection of a Honda XT125 and a 125cc Harley Davidson trail type bike… thinking back I think that’s all there were, in our little group anyway.

We used to venture out over the Severn Bridge to Aust Services on an evening canted over to about 45degrees in an attempt to travel in a straight line in the constant side winds. Aust Services was the place to be seen back then. Apparently.

While on leave one weekend I had my first proper crash: Stupidly I rode into the back of a car, he stopped quicker than me, my front wheel must have made contact ‘cos my forks were a little bit bent out of shape but it was my left shin that took the brunt of it, I had a split in the skin and I could actually see the bone, ouch! No blood though, and yes, I can see my reflection in a mirror.

PC plod turned up and he asked me what happened, of course I told him that I wasn’t really sure as I wasn’t paying attention. Court and 3 points for driving without due care and attention. I must get a better answer for next time…

To make things worse, because I was a member of Her Majesties Forces and I had to go to court I had to have an officer go with me for bringing the army into disrepute.

Anyway a nice nurse stitched me up and I was back on my bike later that day with a slightly wonky set of forks. The handlebars were always facing slightly to the right from that day on.

I even managed a couple of camping trips with this bike, usually with John Whiting on the back seat, so two up, with all our camping gear too. A pair of cheap Oxford throw over panniers and a rucksack was all we needed(beer can be bought anywhere).

One night during the Easter Bank Holiday weekend me and my great friend, brother from another mother, John Whiting were in the bar on a campsite somewhere around Newquay, West Wales when a couple came up to us offering us their car to sleep in that night, we had no idea why, until we got outside. You couldn’t see the tent, there must have been 10 inches of snow covering everything. It didn’t matter though, we just dug our way in and slept the drunken sleep we were expecting.

Another holiday for me, and a first, was when Ian ‘Mo’ Moorhouse and I went to one of the Costas in Spain for my first ever foreign holiday without my parents. I remember the hotel name but not where it was, it was the Hotel Horizonte or something similar. We hired a pair of mopeds while we were there and put a couple of miles on them investigating the locality.

Most of the holiday was spent either getting drunk or being drunk. I drank so much vodka and orange that I couldn’t stand the taste of orange juice for years after.

I got my ear pierced by some girl in the hotel foyer, I still get a lump in there every now and again. Some sort of recurring infection I expect.

After successfully completing my apprenticeship in Chepstow and a further 6 months army training in Gibraltar Barracks, Camberley my bike sat at home idle. I chose to let the train take the strain. 140 miles ride each way seemed like travelling to the ends of the earth and back.

That journey was nothing compared to what was to come.
