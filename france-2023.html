Welcome to my holiday adventures

I originally put this page together using a few photographs from my 2022 holidays, mainly as a way of experimenting with web design.

At the time I thought I was staying at Camping Bois Soleil, not far from Les Sables-d’Olonne in the Vendée region of France I remember it being a very pleasant site in a flat, open part of the country, with good facilities and easy access to the coast.

The site had the usual things you hope for on a French camping holiday: a swimming pool, a bar and restaurant, a small shop and a beach within easy reach. I remember enjoying the stay and coming away with a very positive impression of the place.

The trouble is, this is exactly why I’m glad I’ve started writing these trips down.

Looking back now, I’m no longer completely convinced that Bois Soleil was actually the campsite I stayed at. I have a suspicion it may have been Bois Masson, further north near Saint-Jean-de-Monts. Both are in the Vendée, and after a few years the details have begun to blur together.

That uncertainty is part of the reason for this website. Photos are very good at reminding me what somewhere looked like, but they do not always bring back the smaller details: where I stayed, which road I took, what I discovered nearby, or what made a particular day memorable.

So this page is going to remain a bit of a work in progress. As memories return and I find more photographs, I can add them here and gradually piece the trip back together.

It may not be the most detailed travel diary I’ve ever written, but it is a useful reminder that even the trips I thought I would never forget are already starting to become a little hazy around the edges.

My boat

Surfin’

June 2023 was a sad time for me. I needed to cut my holiday short so that I could attend the funeral of my best friend and brother from a different mother, John Whiting, who had passed away on 4th June.

In the week before I set off for France, I had already travelled north to Leyland to see him in his coffin. It was something I had never done before, but I was very glad that I did. It gave me some private time to reflect on his life, shed a couple of tears, and think about everything he had achieved.

It was a very sad and difficult time, but I was grateful to have had the chance to say goodbye properly and pay my respects.

So, a couple of days earlier than planned, I started my journey back to Leyland for John's funeral. I didn't go home first; I rode straight to Leyland and stayed overnight at the hotel at Charnock Richard services before going to the funeral the following day.

My wife and parents were there too, so we all travelled together to the crematorium.

I did my duty as one of the pallbearers, carrying John on his final journey. Just like he used to do for me on the way back from the pub, although under very different circumstances.
