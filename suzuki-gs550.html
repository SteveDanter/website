1987–1990

Suzuki GS550L

This was my first four-cylinder bike, and yet another purchase from Two Wheel Services.

I honestly can't remember why I chose it. It must already have been about six years old when I bought it; from memory it was a 1981 W-registration bike.

By then I was working for CBSL in Cardiff, so the GS550L quickly became my everyday commuter.

It never missed a beat.

Apparently I Rode Like a Soldier

One day Linda, the receptionist at CBSL, commented on the way I rode.

She said I always seemed to sit very upright on the bike and asked whether that posture had anything to do with my time in the Army.

Nope.

It was just the style of bike.

Linda herself worked on a proper old-fashioned telephone exchange, manually plugging cables into the switchboard to connect incoming calls to the right extension.

Very old technology by today's standards, but it worked perfectly well.

CBSL — Computers Before Computers Were Everywhere

CBSL had actually been quite forward-thinking.

Before I joined, much of the business revolved around providing a payroll bureau service for other companies. Clients would send in their payroll information and staff at CBSL would enter the data into a Burroughs mainframe computer.

Apparently there had even been a round-the-clock shift system to keep the data processing going.

When you think about it, that was pretty advanced for a company founded in 1965, at a time when computers were still enormous, expensive machines and there can't have been many mainframes in use in Britain.

There was also a printing works on site, producing the payslips used by the bureau service and eventually selling printed stationery to other companies too.

When the old Burroughs mainframe finally reached the end of its useful life, there was no dignified retirement.

It was dragged out of the server room by a winch attached to the back of a scrap dealer's lorry.

By then its main value was its weight in scrap metal and whatever gold could be recovered from the electronics.

Technology moves on rather quickly.

From Bureau Service to On-Site Computers

My job was mainly to install cabling and servers at client sites, gradually replacing the old bureau model with systems customers could operate themselves.

So while the GS550L was taking me backwards and forwards to work, I was also unknowingly moving into the career that would keep me occupied for decades.

Crash Number Two

The GS550L was also the bike on which I had my second proper crash.

And, impressively, it happened only about 50 metres from the first one.

I was filtering down the outside of a line of traffic waiting at the Park Street traffic lights when a car suddenly decided to turn right.

I clipped the driver's door and was deflected across the road into the opposite kerb.

I was fine.

The Suzuki was less impressed.

The engine casing was damaged badly enough that it had to be replaced.

Still, compared with seeing the bone in my shin after the previous accident, I counted that one as a result.

My First Motorcycle Trip to France

This was also the bike that took me to France for my first motorcycle camping holiday there.

My cousin Terry came with me on his GS250L.

Neither of us had much idea what we were doing.

We simply loaded the bikes with enough gear for a week and headed off to Northern Brittany.

I genuinely don't remember what we ate for most of the holiday.

I do, however, remember buying the alcohol.

We went into the local supermarket looking for something cider-like and proudly emerged carrying a couple of bottles each.

Back at the tents we settled down, fully prepared for a good drunken evening.

We opened the bottles.

It was disgusting.

Absolutely vile.

If this was what the French drank, I decided, I wasn't sure I ever wanted to come back.

It was only much later that I discovered what we'd actually bought.

Cider vinegar.

I now know what cider vinegar is.

Apparently we didn't then.

We finished it though.

The Bald Tyre Incident

One day I was riding along the dual carriageway past Bridgend Industrial Estate when I was pulled over by the police.

The officer pointed out that my rear tyre was bald.

I already knew.

The bike was being part-exchanged in a couple of days, so in my mind there was absolutely no point in buying a new tyre for somebody else's benefit.

The officer, to his credit, gave me several opportunities to say something sensible along the lines of:

“Yes, officer. I'll get that changed immediately.”

I completely failed to take the hint.

Instead, I kept insisting that I was selling the bike in a couple of days, so what was the point?

Apparently this was not the correct answer.

Another three points.

There does seem to be a theme developing here.

The End of the GS550L

I did sell it shortly afterwards.

Or rather, I think I part-exchanged it.

And, if my memory is right, what replaced it wasn't another bike at all.

It was my MG Metro Turbo.

So for a while, four wheels took over from two.
