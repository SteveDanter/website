The Hyundai Santa Fe

I loved this one too.

Apparently mine was one of the first, if not the first, 2.0 diesel Santa Fes sold in the UK. Whether that's actually true or something I was told at the time and have been repeating ever since, I have no idea, but it's a good story anyway.

Mine was one of the early models with the intercooler breather vent thing on the bonnet. Later versions didn't have it for some reason, which I always thought was a shame because I think it improved the looks. The Santa Fe was otherwise quite a bland-looking car, but underneath it was anything but bland.

It had a sophisticated four-wheel-drive system, plenty of electronic gadgets in the cab and, most importantly for somebody doing the sort of mileage I was doing, it was incredibly comfortable.

I could drive it all day and get out at the other end feeling as though I'd only driven around the block. That's probably just as well, because by the time it was sold three years later it had 160,000 miles on the clock.

I also had a towbar fitted so I could tow my caravan with it. After towing the same caravan to France behind one of my Astras, the Santa Fe barely seemed to notice it was there.

Captain of the ship

One memory from around this time has very little to do with the Santa Fe itself, but it's one of those memories that fixes a particular period of my life in my mind.

We had a Practice Net works outing to the Brecon and Monmouth Canal for a day messing around on the water. I suspect the "boat" we'd hired was the cheaper alternative to hiring a proper narrowboat. It was a fairly basic little thing with a small outboard motor hanging off the back for propulsion.

For reasons that are now lost to history, I was designated pilot and captain.

Thinking about it now, I suspect there was actually a very practical reason for that. I was driving home, so I was probably the only one expected to remain sober.

While I spent the day wrestling with the outboard and attempting to keep us travelling in something resembling a straight line, the rest of the crew were able to concentrate on the more important business of working their way through the free cans of lager and bottles of cheap supermarket wine.

You wouldn't believe how difficult it is to steer a boat in a straight line for hours on end. The canal itself didn't exactly present us with a challenging navigational problem either. We basically travelled along the same stretch of canal until we reached a fish and chip shop, turned around and travelled all the way back again.

Simple.

Except steering the bloody thing was exhausting.

The little outboard had to be physically turned to steer the boat and the forces acting against it were unbelievable. Whether it was torque, gyroscopic forces, the propeller pushing against the water or some mysterious nautical phenomenon known only to people with peaked caps and anchors tattooed on their arms, I don't know.

All I know is that after wrestling with that engine for hours, my biceps were on fire.

Eventually we reached the fish and chip shop, where by now some of the crew were probably having more difficulty steering the chips into their mouths than I was having steering the boat.

We had something to eat, turned the boat around and I got to do the whole thing again in the opposite direction, while my increasingly well-refreshed passengers continued enjoying themselves.

Did I enjoy it? No.

Would I do it again? Definitely.

It was a great laugh — all of us out together, messing around on the water for the day. Looking back, those are often the things I remember most clearly from my years at Practice Net. Not the servers, the computers or whatever problem I'd driven somewhere to fix, but the people and the daft things we occasionally did together.

160,000 miles later

The Santa Fe lasted three years with me and covered 160,000 miles before it was sold. Like the Astras before it, it had spent its life travelling around the country carrying me and whatever equipment I needed, as well as doing family and caravan duties when required.

It may not have been the most exciting-looking car in the world, but it remains one of the cars I remember particularly fondly. Comfortable, capable, dependable and seemingly completely unfazed by the enormous mileage I threw at it.

Still moving.
