Triumph Tiger Sport

My first adventure-style bike was comfortable, capable and lowered sufficiently for my feet to reach the ground—but it came with a mysterious starting problem.

Triumph Tiger 1050 Sport

This was my first adventure-style bike. Naturally, I needed a lowered seat so that I could actually touch the ground. I think I also fitted different suspension links—or “dog bones”—at the rear, which would have lowered it even further.

It’s funny, but I can’t remember a great deal about this bike. I know it was very comfortable and lovely to ride, but I mostly remember the peculiar problem it had with starting when the engine was hot.

If I stopped briefly during a ride—to fill up with petrol, for example—the bike would sometimes behave as though its battery were completely flat. The starter barely wanted to know, so there was no chance of getting the engine running again.

Leave it alone for about 30 minutes, however, and it would start perfectly.

I took it back to Bevans Triumph, where they kept it for a month and changed various parts while trying to get to the bottom of the problem.

While I waited, they lent me a Triumph Speed Twin. That was a very nice little bike to ride. The only problem was that, being retro-styled, it acted as a magnet for men of a certain age. Wherever I stopped, someone would feel compelled to tell me all about the Triumph they had owned as a young man in the 1950s.

Apparently, all Bevans really needed to do to diagnose the Tiger was read the owners’ forums. The problem was well known: the cables connected to the starter motor were a little too skinny. When the engine became hot, their electrical resistance increased and the starter struggled to turn it over.

Fit beefier cables and the problem disappeared.

After all that, the Tiger and I went to France. 🙂

I’m struggling to remember whether I had panniers fitted. If I didn’t, I have no idea how I managed to carry everything. I think I used a cheap Chinese tail bag and strapped all my luggage to the back seat.

Whatever the arrangement was, it must have worked. Both the bike and I made it there and back—although, judging by the photograph, the Tiger may have been carrying most of the responsibility.
