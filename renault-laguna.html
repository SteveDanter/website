Renault Laguna 2.0d Dynamique

Renault Laguna

I bought the Laguna second-hand from Trade Centre Wales. It was about three years old and already had fairly high mileage, which was very much their thing at the time — relatively young cars with plenty of miles already under their wheels, which helped keep the price down.

That suited me perfectly. High mileage had never particularly frightened me. I'd spent years driving company cars into six figures, so getting a three-year-old, high-specification car for considerably less money seemed like a no-brainer.

And this one really was well specified.

It was the Dynamique version and seemed to have just about every toy available. Heated seats, cruise control, electric windows and seats, a sunroof, built-in satnav and even a hard drive that I could load my music onto.

Then there were the HID headlights. Every time I turned them on they performed their little dance, moving up and down and from side to side before settling into position. They also turned with the steering, which seemed remarkably sophisticated at the time. I'm easily pleased — give me a button to press that makes something move electrically and I'll probably be happy for hours.

Underneath all the gadgets was a 2.0-litre diesel engine which suited the car really well. It had plenty of performance, was economical and made the Laguna an excellent long-distance car.

It also ended up towing the caravan. The 2.0 diesel was more than capable of pulling it, although looking back I'm not entirely convinced the rest of the car appreciated the extra workload quite as much as the engine did.

Drive it until it's had enough

I can't remember exactly how many miles were on it when I bought it, but by the time I finally decided it had to go I think the odometer was somewhere around 130,000 miles.

That's not particularly extraordinary mileage by my standards, but the Laguna felt as though it had done considerably more. Nothing especially dramatic had happened to it; it had simply reached that stage where the whole car felt tired.

I'd basically driven it to destruction.

Perhaps all the long journeys and towing the caravan hadn't helped, or perhaps it was simply the combination of its mileage before I bought it and everything I subsequently added. Either way, I knew its time with me was up.

So I took it back to Trade Centre Wales, the same place I'd bought it from, and part-exchanged it.

In return I came home with a Citroën C5 Tourer 3.0 HDi Exclusive.

My favourite car of all time.

But that's another story.

Still moving.
